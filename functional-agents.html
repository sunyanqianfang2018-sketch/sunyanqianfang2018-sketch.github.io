01 / Context & contribution

The work in context

A useful functional agent needs a clear relationship to the work a person is trying to accomplish. This work explored roles and journeys as a starting point for defining AI opportunities in an enterprise setting.

My focus

My focus was the research and service-design foundation for functional agents: role understanding, journey validation and opportunity definition.

  • Role analysis
  • Stakeholder interviews
  • Journey mapping
  • Co-creation
  • Desirability, viability and feasibility
  • Cross-role comparison

02 / Approach

How the work was structured

01

Understand the role

Use role descriptions as a starting point, then bring interviews and workshop feedback into the understanding of goals and responsibilities.

02

Examine the journey

Map phases and more detailed activities to connect needs with the context in which support would be useful. Identify parts of the journey that warrant deeper investigation.

03

Assess opportunities

Cluster possible interventions and discuss desirability, viability and feasibility. Distinguish AI opportunities from needs better addressed through a process or clearer information.

04

Compare across roles

Review overlaps between role-based opportunities to support discussion of shared capabilities, dependencies and potentially duplicated work.

03 / Design reasoning

What the approach makes possible

An opportunity map is not an agent specification or proof of impact. Its value is in connecting a proposed capability to a user need before teams commit to building it.

04 / Outputs & next steps

What the work contributed

  • Role-based personas and journey atlases
  • Opportunity areas and prioritisation criteria
  • Cross-role opportunity mapping
  • Use-case definitions for further assessment

Delivery & follow-up

The case describes discovery and opportunity definition. It does not imply that every proposed agent or capability has been built, validated in production or rolled out.
