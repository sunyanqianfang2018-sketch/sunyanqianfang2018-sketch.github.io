01 / Context & contribution

The work in context

AI adoption involves more than access to a tool. This body of research explored employee expectations, perceived usefulness and the support needed to apply AI to everyday tasks, including emerging agent workflows.

My focus

My focus was understanding AI use through qualitative research and survey analysis, and identifying questions for product experience and adoption support.

  • Employee interviews
  • Survey analysis
  • Thematic synthesis
  • Research question development

02 / Approach

How the work was structured

01

Explore decisions in context

Use interviews to understand the tasks people bring to AI, their expectations and the factors they consider when choosing assistance.

02

Analyse stated needs

Review survey responses and open feedback to organise areas for further investigation. Consider language, task context and the distinction between generating an answer and completing work.

03

Separate types of support

Frame follow-up questions around capability, usability, discoverability and training. Each requires a different response from product and enablement teams.

04

Preserve uncertainty

Distinguish reported preferences from demonstrated task performance. Where a survey does not capture a tool-to-task relationship, treat that relationship as a hypothesis for further research.

03 / Design reasoning

What the approach makes possible

Usage alone cannot explain why someone selects a tool or whether it helps them finish a task. Interviews provide context, while surveys help organise signals; neither should be stretched beyond the questions it actually asked.

04 / Outputs & next steps

What the work contributed

  • Qualitative and survey research summaries
  • A structure for discussing adoption needs
  • Follow-up questions for task-based evaluation

Delivery & follow-up

The work informs product and enablement discussions. Internal adoption levels, employee comments, vendor comparisons and tool-specific assessments are not reproduced here.
